## Literature Evidence

### Intrathecally expanded GZMK+/GZMH+ CD8 T cells targeting EBV antigens may reduce severity of Multiple Sclerosis.

- **Journal:** medRxiv : the preprint server for health sciences
- **Year:** 2025
- **PMID:** [40970118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40970118/)
- **DOI:** 10.1101/2025.08.05.25333071
- **Study type:** Laboratory or other primary study
- **Organizing summary:** PubMed abstract evidence: Combining cerebrospinal fluid B cell receptor and T cell receptor repertoire analysis with transcriptional/ flow cytometry cellular profiles in hundreds of deeply-phenotyped people with Multiple Sclerosis (pwMS) and controls, we identified intrathecal expansion of anti-viral, cytotoxic, granzymes H/K (GZMH+/GZMK+) double positive (DP) CD8+ T cells that recognize EBV epitopes in pwMS.

### Single-cell profiling of peripheral and local immune compartments reveal unique genotype-independent prognostic immune signatures across isocitrate dehydrogenase-stratified glioma.

- **Journal:** Neuro-oncology
- **Year:** 2026
- **PMID:** [40974256](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40974256/)
- **DOI:** 10.1093/neuonc/noaf206
- **Study type:** Human single-cell/transcriptomic study
- **Organizing summary:** PubMed abstract evidence: RESULTS: Our analyses revealed unique intratumoral and peripheral immune cellular ontogenies, including naïve CD4+ T cell enrichment in the IDH-Mut peripheral immune compartment, monocyte enrichment in IDH-WT glioma PBMCs, and emergence of a unique population of GZMH+ CD8+ T cells preferentially in the IDH-Mut microenvironment.

### Comprehensive single-cell transcriptomic profiling of the scalp from patients with moderate-to-severe alopecia areata.

- **Journal:** The Journal of allergy and clinical immunology
- **Year:** 2026
- **PMID:** [41740930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41740930/)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jaci.2026.02.014
- **Study type:** Human single-cell/transcriptomic study
- **Organizing summary:** PubMed abstract evidence: LS AA samples demonstrated robust TH1 activation and cytotoxicity, with upregulated IFNG, GZMH/K, and XCL1/2.
